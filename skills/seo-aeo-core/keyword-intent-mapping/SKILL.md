# Keyword and intent mapping

Turn customer questions and measured search demand into page-level decisions. A cluster represents a coherent user task, not a license to create every keyword variation.

## When to use

Use for new content planning, landing-page mismatch, market expansion and search opportunity analysis. Do not create a fresh URL before checking existing coverage.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Confirmed audience/offers, target market/language, existing URL inventory, business goals and approved research sources. Optional GSC exports, on-site search/support questions and keyword-tool exports. Volume and difficulty are source-specific estimates, not facts about future clicks. Use keyword-intent-map and source-ledger templates; sources G02-G04, G09 and B01.

## Procedure

1. **Define the customer job.** State the decision or action the searcher needs, buyer constraints and business relevance. Separate branded navigation, education, comparison, transaction and support; intent may be mixed.
2. **Collect evidence without inventing demand.** Preserve exact queries from exports and customer language with source/date/market. Add inferred questions only with an explicit hypothesis label. An absent row is not proof of no demand.
3. **Inspect representative results legitimately.** Record query, product, locale, language, date and result formats from permitted tools/manual review. Review actual pages rather than titles alone. Do not run unauthorized automated Google rank scraping.
4. **Cluster by task and expected answer.** Merge synonyms when one good page can satisfy them. Split when audiences, products, locality, format or decision stage require meaningfully different answers. Explain ambiguous assignments rather than hiding them behind a clustering score.
5. **Map to existing pages first.** Compare actual content and conversion path, not URL keywords alone. Designate one intended primary page per coherent cluster; allow a page to satisfy multiple closely related questions. Record overlap with sibling clusters.
6. **Choose a page action.** Keep, improve, create, consolidate or defer. New pages require distinct reader value and proof capacity. A competitor ranking for a term does not establish business fit or a content gap worth pursuing.
7. **Prioritize transparently.** Compare business value, observed demand, ability to contribute original evidence, current coverage, effort and uncertainty. If ranking/CTR assumptions drive a scenario, expose them and calculate with a tool; no fake volume or guaranteed forecast.
8. **Validate with the owner/editor.** Confirm offer alignment, specialist capacity and page boundaries. Deliver the map with research limits and brief-ready priority items. Publishing and URL changes require separate exact approval.

## Artifacts

Completed map with query source, date, locale, intent, confidence, canonical URL, decision, proof, conversion goal and overlap notes. Add a short decision memo explaining the highest-priority cluster and one deliberately rejected opportunity.

## Evidence and unknowns

Use Unknown for absent search volumes or conversion values. Preserve original metrics before normalization. GSC anonymization and export limits can hide queries; customer/support evidence is not a search-volume estimate. Competitor observations are timestamped samples, not permanent SERP truth.

## Pitfalls

Keyword density targets, fixed word counts, one keyword per URL, mechanically translating query lists, mixing countries' volumes and splitting every AI fan-out variant all create misleading plans. A useful FAQ section does not require its own page or guarantee citations.

## Verification

Every recommended page has a distinct customer task, existing-page check, business rationale and evidence source. Every metric retains units/time/market. Conflicting intents and missing inputs are visible. The map can be used to commission a brief without guessing the page's role.
