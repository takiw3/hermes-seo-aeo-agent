# Content briefs

Produce a brief an expert and writer can execute without guessing the audience, proof or acceptance standard. Do not confuse a heading outline with a content strategy.

## When to use

Use before commissioning a new page, substantial rewrite, comparison, category guide or answer asset. If the proposed URL duplicates an existing task, resolve that decision first.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Confirmed business profile, approved page decision/intent map, existing page if any, original proof assets, current source research and reviewer availability. Use content-brief, source-ledger and handoff templates. Sources G02-G04, G09-G10 and B01.

## Procedure

1. **Write the page contract.** Specify target reader, exact task, prior knowledge, locale, canonical destination and business role. State excluded intents and sibling-page boundaries. A secondary keyword is not a reason to change the page's purpose.
2. **Inspect current coverage.** Read the owner's existing page and relevant alternatives. Record evidence dates and result context. Identify missing explanations or decision support without copying competitors' structure or assertions.
3. **Define an original contribution.** Require a named expert insight, worked example, first-hand test, primary dataset, transparent methodology or useful decision tool. If none is available, mark the brief blocked for evidence rather than commissioning generic paraphrase.
4. **Build the claim plan.** For each material promise, statistic, price or recommendation, assign an inspected source or an evidence-collection task and reviewer. Separate business-confirmed facts, official documentation, inference and opinion. Time-sensitive claims get a freshness trigger.
5. **Design the structure by reader need.** Draft a truthful title, answer/promise, logical sections, comparison dimensions, caveats, examples and next step. Specify why each section exists. Avoid fixed word counts, keyword-density targets and a mandatory FAQ schema instruction.
6. **Specify implementation details.** Include title/meta description proposals, internal incoming/outgoing links, image purpose/rights/alt guidance, accessibility, visible author/reviewer details and a schema candidate only if applicable and currently supported.
7. **Match the CTA to intent.** Educational visitors may need a practical next resource; transactional visitors need accurate offer details. Confirm claims, prices, availability and qualification criteria with the owner.
8. **Set quality gates.** Define sufficient evidence, factual/specialist review, originality, useful completeness, mobile/rendered QA and conversion-path tests. Record critical blockers and who can resolve them.
9. **Deliver a reviewable brief.** Complete the template, label unresolved fields Unknown and show the commissioning decision. Brief approval does not authorize CMS creation, publishing, outreach or future scheduling.

## Artifacts

Versioned brief, claim-source ledger, evidence requests and a handoff with owner/reviewer, dependencies and acceptance tests. The writer should know what to omit as well as what to include.

## Evidence and unknowns

A source title or search snippet is not enough to approve a claim. First-hand experience must identify who performed the work and its limits. Do not invent customer quotes, screenshots or commercial data to satisfy a brief. Competitor assertions can frame a question, not validate a business claim.

## Pitfalls

Avoid briefs that only list keywords, require every competitor heading, fabricate personas, optimize for an imagined AI passage length, or create production commitments without reviewer capacity. Regulated advice needs an appropriately qualified reviewer, not an AI confidence score.

## Verification

The brief has one coherent task, differentiated proof, claim ownership, page boundaries, a useful CTA and testable acceptance criteria. All required evidence exists or is explicitly blocking. The owner can approve scope without unknowingly authorizing publication.
